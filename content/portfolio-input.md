### Dynamics of an Insulated Rail Joint
Slug: [rail-joint-dynamics]
Type: [Academic]
Year: []
Role: [Candidate, exam project]
Tags: [FEA, ANSYS APDL, Python, Structural Dynamics, Signal Processing, Damage Detection]
Featured: [yes]
Cover image: [cover.png]
Images: [fe-mesh.png | Finite element mesh of the insulated rail joint]
Links: [Presentation | assets/docs/rail-joint-presentation.pdf]
Summary: [3D transient FE model of a moving Hertzian load on an insulated rail joint, with a Python spectral index that detects end-post failure from a 360 to 330 Hz frequency shift.]
Details:
Exam project for *Principles of Machine Design with FEM Applications*, Sant'Anna School of Advanced Studies (Istituto di Intelligenza Meccanica).

**Problem.** An insulated rail joint keeps continuous welded rails mechanically continuous while electrically isolated for signalling circuits. A broken insulating end-post causes short circuits, which give false "clear" signals (collision risk) or false "occupied" signals (network blockage). The goal was to reconstruct the strain wave $\varepsilon_x(x,t)$ from probes at least 100 mm apart and to detect an end-post failure.

**Model.**
- Hertzian train load of $F_z = 150$ kN and $F_x = 30$ kN travelling at 8 m/s, on an elastically supported rail with fishplates and an insulating end-post.
- Geometry partitioned in SpaceClaim so nodes align exactly with 22 strain probes (76.43 mm spacing). Hex-dominant mesh with 2.5 mm refinement at the load path (to resolve the 10 mm minor axis of the contact ellipse) and a coarse 16 mm swept mesh on the rail base.
- Moving load scripted in APDL, since Workbench cannot apply a pressure that varies in space and time. SURF154 surface elements carry the load, with a spatial bounding-box filter to cut the computation. The elastic foundation uses COMBIN14 springs.
- Linear transient analysis with a fixed 0.2 ms time step (2.5 kHz Nyquist limit), 25 steps across the contact patch, and HHT integration to suppress numerical noise.

**Verification.**
- Modal analysis of the first 25 modes: four semi-rigid modes, then local modes. Less than 1 Hz variation between meshes, and the damaged end-post does not shift the natural frequencies.
- Mesh versus wavelength check: even the worst case (16 mm elements on bending waves) gives about 56 elements per wavelength, against a requirement of 4 to 6.

**Results.**
- Wave reconstruction by cubic spline interpolation in Python. The undamaged and damaged cases differ by about $\pm200\,\mu\varepsilon$, confined to the fishplate region ($\pm0.25$ m), where the damaged joint opens and closes like a compliant connection.
- Welch power spectral density shows the low-frequency modes unaffected, and a clear shift from **360 Hz to 330 Hz** caused by the lost local stiffness. The shift survives normalisation by the load spectrum.
- A diagnostic index over the 310 to 380 Hz band returns about **500** for complete failure ($DI \gg 1$ means failure), and lower values would indicate partial damage.

### Aerodynamics of a VTOL Rotor
Slug: [vtol-rotor-aerodynamics]
Type: [Academic]
Year: [2025]
Role: [BS thesis researcher]
Tags: [CFD, Rotor Aerodynamics, VTOL, UAV, Compressible Flow, Automation]
Featured: [yes]
Cover image: [cover.png]
Images: []
Links: []
Summary: [Hover CFD of a 5 m VTOL rotor blade across two airfoils, two diameters and five pitch angles, selecting the configuration that delivers 1500 N of thrust at 600 rpm with the least power.]
Details:
Bachelor's thesis in collaboration with SkyEye Systems (project Vertigo) and the Laboratory of Experimental Aerodynamics, University of Pisa.

**Goal.** Compare the hover performance of a large-scale VTOL rotor for two airfoils (V1, V2), two diameters (4500 and 5000 mm) and five pitch angles (0 to 8°). The design point is **1500 N of thrust at 600 rpm**. Preliminary runs showed that diameters below 4500 mm cannot meet the requirement.

**Setup.**
- Steady RANS with a coupled solver, compressible ideal gas and SST $k$-$\omega$ turbulence. The tip Mach number is about 0.41 to 0.45, above the $M<0.3$ incompressibility threshold, so compressibility is modelled.
- Rotating region at 600 rpm inside a prismatic domain extending 15 m above and below the blades, with ground friction and quiescent velocity-inlet boundaries.
- 8 to 9 million trimmed cells with 8 prism layers and $y^+\approx0.5$ to 30.
- The simulation pipeline is fully automated, which produces the thrust, torque and power maps across all configurations.

**Results.**
- Both airfoils reach the 1500 N requirement, at different pitch angles. Required power is similar, with the V2 saving about 200 W.
- The optimum is the **V2 airfoil at $D=5000$ mm and 6.42° pitch**.
- The spanwise Mach number matches the analytical estimate near the root, but the CFD peak is higher. CFD is also the only way to locate the Mach maximum along the span, because the estimate ignores 3D flow effects.
- Spanwise lift and drag coefficients were computed from the local asymptotic velocity seen by each blade section.

### Critical Engine Speed in an Engine Connecting Rod
Slug: [connecting-rod-critical-speed]
Type: [Academic]
Year: [2026]
Role: [Project author]
Tags: [FEA, Ansys, Siemens NX, Contact Mechanics, Bolted Joints, Machine Design]
Featured: [yes]
Cover image: []
Images: []
Links: [Project report | assets/docs/connecting-rod-report.pdf]
Summary: [Analytical and nonlinear contact FEA prediction of the engine speed at which inertia loads open the big-end joint of a TVR 3.5 L connecting rod: 8415 rpm by FEM against 8770 rpm analytically.]
Details:
Machine Design course project (supervisors Prof. Francesco Frendo and Prof. Andrea Chiocca, academic year 2025/2026).

**Problem.** For the 3.5 L TVR AJP6 straight-six (350 hp T350 version), find the engine speed $N_{crit}$ at which the tensile inertia load at top dead centre starts to separate the connecting rod from its big-end cap. Separation is defined as zero contact pressure at any point of the interface. The engine was chosen because its original blueprints are public, so the rod was rebuilt from original drawings. The joint has two M10×1.25 class 12.9 screws (preloaded to 75% of yield, 50.5 kN each) and two hollow dowels.

**Analytical model.**
- Planar slider-crank at constant speed, with TDC acceleration $a\simeq-R\,\omega^2(1+\lambda)$ and $\lambda=R/L=0.2737$.
- Equivalent reciprocating mass of 600 g. Lever effect on the bolts ($c=7$ mm, $e=33.5$ mm), plus clamped-section and screw stiffnesses of 863 and 420 kN/mm, so the screws carry a fraction $\xi=0.327$ of the external load.
- Separation occurs when the clamping force reaches zero: $N_{crit}\simeq8770$ rpm.

**FEM model.**
- Parametric CAD in Siemens NX with the WAVE geometry linker. Prepared in Ansys SpaceClaim, using a quarter model thanks to two symmetry planes, and partitions that give a conformal, mostly hexahedral mesh with 0.5 mm refinement at the rod-cap contact.
- Crankpin support modelled as a zero-displacement 120° arc, with the truncated rod and piston replaced by a 480 g point mass.
- Five contact pairs (frictional, $\mu=0.2$, or bonded). The bolt preload is applied in load step 1, then a MAPDL pseudo-time ramp of the rotational acceleration sweeps the engine speed in load step 2. This finds $N_{crit}$ in a single solve, using minimum contact pressure and contact status as separation criteria.

**Results.**
- $N_{crit,FEM}\simeq$ **8415 rpm**, which is **4.05%** from the analytical value.
- The constant-eccentricity assumption is confirmed, since the deformation is 0.06 mm against $e=33.5$ mm. Neglecting the dowel is also justified: it carries about 0.75 kN, or 2.9% of the inertia load.
- Mesh independence demonstrated over four global refinements (150%, 100%, 80%, 60%) by solving the whole RPM ramp for each.
- Future work: dowel interference fit, detailed threads and bearing, full engine cycle, and the crankshaft and crankpin contact.
